# Environmental Microbial Ecology Review Framework

## Question and Contribution

- Can the research question, knowledge gap, hypotheses, and novelty each be stated in one sentence?
- Does the Introduction narrow from the biogeochemical problem to the unresolved ecological mechanism?
- Are predictions distinguishable from post hoc interpretations?
- Is novelty visible in the Abstract findings, final Introduction paragraph, and Discussion synthesis?

## Claim-Evidence Ledger

For each central claim, record:

| Claim | Section | Evidence layer | Figure/data source | Inference level | Alternative explanation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Evidence layers are not interchangeable:

| Evidence | Supports | Does not alone support |
|---|---|---|
| Amplicon ASVs | composition, diversity, turnover, observed sample distribution | activity, pathways, species novelty |
| Metagenomic reads | relative gene/taxon profiles and distributional associations | transcript activity or process rate |
| MAGs | genome-resolved taxonomy and metabolic potential | realized activity; confident absence in incomplete genomes |
| Transcripts/proteins | expression under sampled conditions | process rate without calibration |
| Correlation/RDA/Mantel/MRM | association and covariation | causal environmental control |
| NST/null models | model-dependent assembly signatures | proof of deterministic or stochastic mechanism |
| Incubations/rates | activity under tested conditions | unrestricted field-wide causality |

## Six Review Lenses

1. **Logic:** question, hypotheses, novelty, argument continuity, conclusion support.
2. **Methods:** design, replication, compositionality, model assumptions, multiple testing, reproducibility.
3. **Mechanism:** biological plausibility, alternative explanations, evidence-layer boundaries.
4. **Style:** terminology, cautious wording, journal conventions, concise transitions.
5. **Figures:** one question per figure, panel-text agreement, legends, sample size, tests, SI/dataset traceability.
6. **Adversarial:** strongest counterargument, confounding, circular reasoning, information stacking, likely rejection grounds.

## Mechanistic Discussion Structure

For each mechanism, write in this order:

1. Observation from the study.
2. Evidence-bounded interpretation.
3. Relevant literature comparison.
4. Plausible alternative explanation.
5. Decisive future test.

Remove generic ecosystem importance statements that do not explain a reported result.

## Terminology

- Prefer `associated with`, `covaried with`, `consistent with`, and `provides a hypothesis` for observational results.
- Reserve `drive`, `regulate`, `control`, `adaptation`, and `mechanism` for identifying designs or direct tests.
- Italicize gene symbols and genus/species names; follow the target journal for higher taxonomic ranks.
- Avoid inflated modifiers unless quantitatively justified.

## Editorial Risk Test

A major concern must identify a specific claim, why current evidence is insufficient, where the problem occurs, and the minimum corrective action. Do not inflate a stylistic preference into a major scientific defect.
